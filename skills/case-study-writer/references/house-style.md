# Case-study house style

Use this default for GTME-authored work unless the user supplies another style.

## Structure

**Overview:** the client and its starting situation, the engagement objective, and the strongest supported outcome.

**The Challenge:** the concrete workflow/data problem and its evidenced cost. Avoid blaming individuals.

**The Strategy:** numbered workstreams describing what was built and how the client uses it. Name tools when they clarify the mechanism.

**The Results:** measured before/after values, delivered capabilities, and adoption evidence. Keep these categories distinct. Include a testimonial only when an authentic, approved quote is available.

**TLDR:** four to seven concise takeaways consistent with the body.

## Voice and naming

For GTME named versions, use “The GTM Engineering Company” on first mention, then “we” where appropriate. For another authoring brand, use its configured name. The client remains the subject of the outcome.

Use short paragraphs, concrete verbs, and measured claims. Prefer colons, semicolons, or separate sentences to dashes as prose punctuation in the GTME style. Avoid hype and exclamation marks. Hyphens in words and numeric ranges are fine.

A useful default is 600–900 words, with an operations edition up to approximately 1,100 where a before/after table adds value. The user's requested length takes precedence. Do not pad a thin evidence base to reach a target.

## Evidence

Maintain a private claim-to-source map. Never pluralize one booked meeting into “meetings,” turn an enabled workflow into demonstrated ROI, or imply unmeasured downstream results. Represent completion status and material limitations accurately. Leave private personnel, contract, pricing, and internal-conflict details out of marketing copy unless explicitly authorized and relevant.
